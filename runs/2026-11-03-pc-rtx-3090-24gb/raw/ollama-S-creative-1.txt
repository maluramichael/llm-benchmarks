Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his routine unbroken by modern noise. One misty Tuesday, while mending a frayed rope near the harbor's edge, he pulled a glass bottle from the surf. Inside, rolled tightly in newspaper, lay a note with no sender listed. Curiosity tugged at him; he unfolded it under the flickering lighthouse light.

The handwriting was fresh, written by a stranger named Leo. It detailed a tragedy Elias had only heard whispers about—a shipwreck twenty years prior, blamed on his negligence. Elias read every word, his heart heavy with sudden, crushing guilt. He realized the bottle wasn't just a message; it was an indictment. Without hesitation, he tore the paper into shreds and tossed them back into the churning waves, letting the saltwater wash over the ink before they could dry. The ocean would take its secrets again.
