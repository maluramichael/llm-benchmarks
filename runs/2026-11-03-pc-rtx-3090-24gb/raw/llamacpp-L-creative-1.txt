Elias wiped the salt spray from his goggles, the lighthouse beam slicing through the fog like a silver blade. For thirty years, his world had been the rhythmic *thump-thump* of the waves and the creak of the iron stairs. But that morning, the tide dragged something peculiar onto the rocks: a green glass bottle, heavy with sand.

He pried the cork open with trembling fingers. Inside, a rolled parchment unfolded. The ink was faded, the handwriting shaky, dated 1942.

*"If you are reading this, the war is over. I hope the sky is clear. I hope you are free."*

Elias stared at the words, the date burning his eyes. He had never spoken of the brother he’d lost in the war, a silence that had grown heavy as the stones around him. A single tear cut a track through the grime on his cheek. He walked to the lantern room, not to light the beacon, but to look out. For the first time in decades, the fog had lifted, revealing a sky so vast and blue it felt like a promise kept. He held the bottle tight, the glass warm in his cold hands, and whispered, "I am free."
