This code implements the **Bubble Sort** algorithm. It sorts a list of numbers in ascending order by repeatedly swapping adjacent elements if they are in the wrong order.

### How it works (Plain English):
Imagine you have a row of people, and you want them lined up from shortest to tallest. You walk down the line looking at neighbors:
1. If the person on the left is taller than the person on their right, you swap them so the shorter one is now on the left.
2. You keep walking down the line until you reach the end.
3. Then, you go back to the very beginning and repeat the process.
4. You keep doing this round after round until no more swaps are needed. At that point, everyone is perfectly sorted.

The nested loops in your code do exactly this:
- The outer loop (`i`) controls how many "passes" through the list you make. Each pass pushes the largest unsorted element to its correct position at the end.
- The inner loop (`j`) looks at every pair of neighbors and swaps them if needed.

### Input: `[3, 1, 2]`

Let's trace it step-by-step:

**Pass 1 (i = 0):**
- Compare index 0 and 1: `3` vs `1`. Since `3 > 1`, swap them. List becomes `[1, 3, 2]`.
- Compare index 1 and 2: `3` vs `2`. Since `3 > 2`, swap them. List becomes `[1, 2, 3]`.
- End of Pass 1. The largest number (3) is now in the correct spot at the end.

**Pass 2 (i = 1):**
- Compare index 0 and 1: `1` vs `2`. Since `1 < 2`, no swap needed. List remains `[1, 2, 3]`.
- End of Pass 2. The list is now fully sorted.

**Pass 3 (i = 2):**
- The inner loop range becomes empty (`len(xs) - 1 - i` = `3 - 1 - 2` = `0`). No comparisons happen.

### Final Output:
The function returns the sorted list:
```python
[1, 2, 3]
```
