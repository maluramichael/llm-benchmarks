Elias wiped the salt spray from his goggles, the wind howling its eternal lullaby against the granite base of the tower. The island was a speck in the vast, churning ocean, and for thirty years, his only conversation partners had been the creaking gears and the distant gulls. But today, the tide brought something different.

Wedged between the barnacled rocks was a bottle, green as deep sea glass, swirling with sand. Elias’s heart hammered against his ribs. He pulled it free, uncorking it with trembling fingers. The paper inside was yellowed, brittle, and smelled of old ink and sorrow.

*“If you are reading this, I am finally gone. I left the light burning for you. Do not let it go out.”*

No signature. No date. Just a plea that felt less like a message and more like a command. Elias looked up at the dark sky, then at the beam of light cutting through the rain. He realized with a chilling clarity that the keeper’s logbook had been missing the previous night. He hadn’t written the entry. He had forgotten. The bottle wasn’t a relic; it was a warning from his own future self, or perhaps a ghost from a past he couldn’t quite remember. He tightened his grip on the bottle, the light spinning steadily above him, refusing to dim.
