To find the number of positive integers smaller than 1000 that are divisible by 7 but not divisible by 11, we can use the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion.

Let $S$ be the set of positive integers smaller than 1000. So, $S = \{1, 2, 3, \dots, 999\}$.
Let $A$ be the set of numbers in $S$ divisible by 7.
Let $B$ be the set of numbers in $S$ divisible by 11.
We want to find the number of elements in $A$ that are not in $B$, which is $|A| - |A \cap B|$.

**Step 1: Calculate $|A|$ (numbers divisible by 7)**
The largest multiple of 7 less than or equal to 999 is $\lfloor \frac{999}{7} \rfloor = 142$.
So, there are 142 positive integers divisible by 7.
$|A| = 142$.

**Step 2: Calculate $|A \cap B|$ (numbers divisible by both 7 and 11)**
A number divisible by both 7 and 11 must be divisible by their least common multiple, $\text{lcm}(7, 11) = 77$.
The largest multiple of 77 less than or equal to 999 is $\lfloor \frac{999}{77} \rfloor$.
$999 \div 77 \approx 12.97$, so the quotient is 12.
So, there are 12 positive integers divisible by 77.
$|A \cap B| = 12$.

**Step 3: Calculate the final result**
The number of integers divisible by 7 but not by 11 is:
$|A| - |A \cap B| = 142 - 12 = 130$.

ANSWER: 130
