Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his routine unbroken by the crashing waves or the biting wind. One gale-ravaged afternoon, while sweeping the gallery, he found it wedged beneath the iron railing: a heavy glass bottle.

Inside, a note trembled on the salt-slicked glass. "To the Keeper of Light: The stars are faint tonight, but your beam cuts through the dark. Do not fear the storm; you are the anchor. - A stranger from the mainland."

Elias paused, his oil lamp flickering against the sudden chill. He had never received such words before. Usually, the sea only offered rusted keys or discarded toys. His heart swelled with a warmth he hadn't felt in decades. He placed the bottle gently back into the water, feeling a strange kinship with the currents. Perhaps, just perhaps, someone far away was reminded of him now, finding safety in his light. As the moon broke through the clouds, Elias adjusted the lens, letting the golden beam sweep the horizon once more, no longer just a duty, but a connection.
