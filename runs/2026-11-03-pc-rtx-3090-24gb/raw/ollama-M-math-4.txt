To find how many positive integers smaller than 1000 are divisible by 7 but **not** divisible by 11, we proceed in steps:

---

### Step 1: Count numbers divisible by 7 less than 1000

We want to find how many integers $ n $ satisfy:

$$
n < 1000 \quad \text{and} \quad n \equiv 0 \pmod{7}
$$

The largest multiple of 7 less than 1000 is:

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{999}{7} \right\rfloor = 142
$$

So, there are **142** positive integers less than 1000 that are divisible by 7.

---

### Step 2: Count numbers divisible by both 7 and 11 (i.e., divisible by 77)

We now subtract the numbers divisible by **both** 7 and 11, since we want numbers divisible by 7 **but not** by 11.

The least common multiple of 7 and 11 is:

$$
\text{lcm}(7, 11) = 77
$$

Now, count how many multiples of 77 are less than 1000:

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{999}{77} \right\rfloor = 12
$$

So, there are **12** numbers less than 1000 that are divisible by both 7 and 11.

---

### Step 3: Subtract to get the final count

$$
142 - 12 = 130
$$

---

**ANSWER: 130**
