To solve the problem, we need to count how many **positive integers less than 1000** are **divisible by 7** but **not divisible by 11**.

---

### Step 1: Count numbers less than 1000 divisible by 7

We want to find how many integers $ n $ satisfy:

$$
n < 1000 \quad \text{and} \quad n \equiv 0 \pmod{7}
$$

The largest multiple of 7 less than 1000 is:

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{999}{7} \right\rfloor = 142
$$

So, there are **142** positive integers less than 1000 that are divisible by 7.

---

### Step 2: Count numbers less than 1000 divisible by both 7 and 11

These are the numbers divisible by the **least common multiple (LCM)** of 7 and 11.

$$
\text{LCM}(7, 11) = 77
$$

Now, count how many multiples of 77 are less than 1000:

$$
\left\lfloor \frac{999}{77} \right\rfloor = 12
$$

So, there are **12** numbers less than 1000 that are divisible by both 7 and 11.

---

### Step 3: Subtract to get the count of numbers divisible by 7 but **not** by 11

$$
142 - 12 = 130
$$

---

### Final Answer:

ANSWER: 130
