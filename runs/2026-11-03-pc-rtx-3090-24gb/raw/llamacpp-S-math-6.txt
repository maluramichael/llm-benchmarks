To find the remainder when $3^{101}$ is divided by 7, we can use the properties of modular arithmetic and patterns in powers.

1.  **Calculate the first few powers of 3 modulo 7:**
    *   $3^1 \equiv 3 \pmod{7}$
    *   $3^2 = 9 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$
    *   $3^3 = 3 \times 2 = 6 \equiv 6 \pmod{7}$ (or $-1$)
    *   $3^4 = 3 \times 6 = 18 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$
    *   $3^5 = 3 \times 4 = 12 \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$
    *   $3^6 = 3 \times 5 = 15 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$

2.  **Identify the cycle:**
    The remainders repeat in a cycle of length 6: $\{3, 2, 6, 4, 5, 1\}$.
    Specifically, $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$. This is consistent with Fermat's Little Theorem, which states that for a prime $p$, $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ if $a$ is not divisible by $p$. Here $p=7$, so $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$.

3.  **Simplify the exponent:**
    We need to find $3^{101} \pmod{7}$.
    Since $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$, we can write $3^{101}$ as:
    $3^{101} = 3^{6 \times 16 + 5} = (3^6)^{16} \times 3^5$
    
    Taking modulo 7:
    $(3^6)^{16} \times 3^5 \equiv 1^{16} \times 3^5 \equiv 1 \times 3^5 \equiv 3^5 \pmod{7}$

4.  **Calculate the final value:**
    From our earlier calculation, $3^5 \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$.
    
    Alternatively, using negative residues:
    $3^3 \equiv -1 \pmod{7}$
    $3^{101} = 3^{3 \times 33 + 2} = (3^3)^{33} \times 3^2$
    $\equiv (-1)^{33} \times 3^2 \pmod{7}$
    $\equiv -1 \times 9 \pmod{7}$
    $\equiv -9 \pmod{7}$
    $\equiv -2 \pmod{7}$
    $\equiv 5 \pmod{7}$

Both methods yield a remainder of 5.

ANSWER: 5
