Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his days measured by the rhythm of the tide. One stormy evening, while scrubbing the gallery window, he spotted a rusted iron key wedged beneath a loose stone. Curious, he pulled it free, only for the key to slide silently into his pocket, vanishing into the dark.

Weeks turned to months. The lighthouse remained silent, save for the groan of the wind. Until the day the key began to hum, a low vibration that resonated through Elias's bones. He rushed to the lantern room, where the key now glowed with a soft, golden light. Suddenly, the heavy iron door of the base room clicked open.

Inside, floating in a glass jar suspended in the air, was a single, perfect hourglass. The sand within wasn't moving; instead, it flowed upward, forming tiny, intricate stars that spiraled toward the ceiling. Elias realized with a gasp: time had not stopped for the world outside, but here, in the heart of the lighthouse, it had been rewritten entirely. He was no longer just a keeper; he was the custodian of an endless now.
