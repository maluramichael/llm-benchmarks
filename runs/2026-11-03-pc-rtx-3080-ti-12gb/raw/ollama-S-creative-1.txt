Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his days measured by the rhythm of crashing waves and the shifting light of oil lamps. He knew every cove, every storm, and every whisper of the sea. That changed on a grey Tuesday when a small, chipped glass bottle tumbled from a gull's beak into his drying room.

Inside, tucked beneath rusted tin foil, was a note written in fresh, frantic ink: *"If you read this, turn off your light for one hour. I'm hiding the truth about the storm coming."*

Elias froze, the oil lamp trembling in his hand. For decades, he had refused to believe anyone could warn him when the ocean roared. Yet, the handwriting looked familiar—not like a stranger's script, but like his own father's, long since lost at sea. A chill ran down his spine as the wind howled outside, matching the urgency in the paper. He wiped his forehead and stared at the bottle, weighing isolation against the terrifying possibility of saving lives he couldn't yet see.
