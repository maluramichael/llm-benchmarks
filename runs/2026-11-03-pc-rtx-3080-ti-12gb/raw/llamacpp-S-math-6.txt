1.  $3^1 \equiv 3 \pmod{7}$
2.  $3^2 = 9 \equiv 2 \pmod{7}$
3.  $3^3 = 3^2 \cdot 3 \equiv 2 \cdot 3 = 6 \equiv -1 \pmod{7}$
4.  $3^4 = 3^3 \cdot 3 \equiv -1 \cdot 3 = -3 \equiv 4 \pmod{7}$
5.  $3^5 = 3^4 \cdot 3 \equiv 4 \cdot 3 = 12 \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$
6.  $3^6 = 3^5 \cdot 3 \equiv 5 \cdot 3 = 15 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$

We observe that $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$. This means the powers of 3 repeat in a cycle of length 6. Specifically, for any integer $k$, $3^{6k} \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$.

$101 = 6 \times 16 + 5$

Using the property of exponents $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ and the fact that $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$:
$3^{101} = 3^{6 \times 16 + 5} = (3^6)^{16} \cdot 3^5$

Substitute $3^6 \equiv 1 \pmod{7}$:
$(3^6)^{16} \cdot 3^5 \equiv 1^{16} \cdot 3^5 \pmod{7}$
$1^{16} \cdot 3^5 \equiv 1 \cdot 3^5 \pmod{7}$
$3^5 \pmod{7}$

From our earlier calculation, we know that $3^5 \equiv 5 \pmod{7}$.

Therefore, the remainder when $3^{101}$ is divided by 7 is 5.
