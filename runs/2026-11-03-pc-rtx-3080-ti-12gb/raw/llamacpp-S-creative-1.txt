Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his days measured by the rhythm of the tide. One stormy evening, while scrubbing the gallery window, he found a bottle wedged between the iron grates. Inside, a folded piece of paper fluttered in the sea salt.

It wasn't a love letter or a plea for help, but a map marked with a specific, forgotten coordinate on the mainland. Elias traced the route with a trembling hand. The coordinates led not to a city, but to the hidden garden of an old friend he had never visited in decades.

With a lantern in hand, he followed the trail through the crashing waves, ignoring the warnings of the local fishermen. When the storm finally broke, he arrived at a small cottage, its door unlocked and welcoming. Inside, his childhood friend, now gray and frail, looked up from her sewing.

"You're late," she whispered, her eyes filling with tears. "I was waiting exactly this long."
