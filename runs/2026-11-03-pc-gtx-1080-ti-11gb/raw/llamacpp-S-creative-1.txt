Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his days measured by the rhythm of the tide. One stormy evening, while scrubbing the gallery window, he found a bottle wedged between the iron grates. Inside, a folded piece of paper fluttered in the sea salt.

It wasn't a love letter or a plea for help, but a map marked with a specific coordinate near the island's southern tip. Elias stared at the ink, his heart hammering against his ribs. For decades, he had lived in solitude, guarding the light for strangers. This message suggested someone had been waiting for him, perhaps a long-lost relative or a traveler seeking guidance.

He unfolded the letter further, revealing a date from twenty years ago. A name appeared: "Sarah," the name of the girl who had once stood beside him before the wind took her. Elias's hands trembled as he traced the words, the cold wind suddenly feeling less biting. He was no longer just a keeper; he was a man who had found his way home.
