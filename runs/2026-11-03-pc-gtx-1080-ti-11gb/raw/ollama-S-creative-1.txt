Elias had tended the same jagged rock for forty years, his days measured by the rhythm of crashing waves and the steady drumming of gulls. He was old, his hands rough like bark, until one stormy evening he found something washed ashore near his keep. It wasn't trash or driftwood, but a glass bottle sealed with wax, bearing no name yet etched with precise coordinates pointing directly to his lighthouse.

Inside floated a single sheet of paper: *"The light seen here does not guide ships; it reveals the truth that everyone is lost."* Elias sat by the window as the fog rolled in, the beam cutting through the gray mist. For the first time in decades, he looked out beyond the horizon and felt not the solitude of duty, but a profound, vibrating loneliness. He realized then that the light was indeed finding him, seeking him, waiting for a keeper who could finally understand.
